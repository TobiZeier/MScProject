Measuring and Prioritising Technical Debt in Mission-Critical Trading Systems
A TOPSIS framework on live ITSM and CMDB data from a regulated Swiss trading institution
Tobias Zeier · MSc Enterprise IT Management, University of Essex
STUDENT ID 12696372 · SUPERVISOR DR ZAHID ULLAH · CO-SUPERVISOR DOUG MILLWARD · 12 JULY 2026

0:00–0:45. This chart is the thesis in one image: six production trading services, ranked by technical debt priority, computed entirely from operational records. No source code was read to produce it. The next 15 minutes explain why that matters, how it was built, and why you can trust it.

The problem lives outside the code

MOTIVATION

  • In vendor-dominated trading IT, source code is inaccessible, so the dominant measurement tools (SonarQube, SQALE, CAST) are structurally inapplicable
  • The debt that matters here sits in infrastructure lifecycle exposure, patch delay and operational instability, which code-centric tools cannot observe
  • The consequences are measurable and compound:
    • 10% more technical debt → −16% gross return on assets in COTS platforms (Banker, Liang and Ramasubbu, 2020)
    • one month of patch delay → 4.9× higher odds of compromise; three months → 9.1× (Tizio, Armellini and Massacci, 2023)
    • repaying architectural debt cut incidents by 84% in production microservices (de Toledo et al., 2021)
The costliest debt in this environment is invisible to the standard tools, but its symptoms are recorded daily in ITSM and CMDB systems.

0:45–2:00. Frame the environment: continuous availability, FINMA oversight, vendor platforms that cannot be refactored. Then the three anchor numbers, delivered slowly. The last line sets up the whole design: the symptoms are already being recorded, we just need an instrument that reads them.

The gap and the research questions

RQ · CHAPTER 1–2

Across 44 primary studies on TD prioritisation, quantitative multi-criteria approaches are uncommon and industrial validation is rare (Lenarduzzi et al., 2021). No reviewed study combines service-level indicators, multi-criteria prioritisation, live operational data and a trading IT context.

RQ1
How can technical debt in mission-critical trading systems be prioritised quantitatively at the service level using operational data?
RQ2
To what extent do operational service management metrics serve as valid proxies for technical debt severity?
RQ3
How stable are the resulting rankings when criterion weights are perturbed?

2:00–3:00. Emphasise the four-way combination: each pairwise element exists in the literature, the combination does not. RQ2 is deliberately worded as “to what extent”: the proxy link is treated as a testable hypothesis throughout, not a premise. That framing recurs on the evaluation slides.

Design: five indicators, one auditable instrument

METHOD · DSR

Design Science Research (Hevner et al., 2004); single-organisation embedded case study; unit of analysis = the IT service as a configuration item.

Indicator Source Debt dimension (Table 2.3)

Mean time to restore (MTTR) ITSM Infrastructure / platform

Dimension assignments are this study’s literature-grounded synthesis. Documentation, test coverage and coupling indicators were excluded on data availability, not relevance.

3:00–4:15. Walk one row fully (MTTR: Forsgren et al. validate the metric, Nord et al. ground its diagnostic reading as debt impeding recovery from change). State the exclusions and why: honesty about what could not be measured is part of the audit trail. Pre-empts “why these five?”.

From records to ranking

METHOD · TOPSIS

Pipeline

  1. Extract indicators from live ITSM/CMDB records
  2. Vector normalisation (preserves extreme observations)
  3. Weighting: equal (0.20 each) and differential (0.30 MTTR & patch recency)
  4. TOPSIS closeness coefficient Ci ∈ [0, 1]
  5. Rank: lowest Ci = highest debt priority

Why TOPSIS

  • Handles mixed cost/benefit criteria and different scales in one procedure
  • Produces a full ranking, unlike VIKOR’s compromise set or AHP alone
  • Precedent: Albarak et al. (2022) for TD, but on controlled data
  • Method choice treated as a robustness question, tested against SAW and VIKOR

4:15–5:15. Keep this brisk; examiners know TOPSIS. The two points to land: differential weights are a declared practitioner assumption, not literature-derived, and are themselves stress-tested; and method choice is not defended a priori but empirically, which is where the interesting findings come from.

The data, and its honest limits

DATA · CHAPTER 4

  • Portfolio of 108 services; 53 small vendor GUIs excluded; 6 selected for demonstration on data completeness over the full window
  • Jan 2024 – Dec 2025, production records only, anonymised at source
  • Quality filters: alarm-storm consolidation, <0.1 h auto-closures removed, 98th percentile MTTR cap

Declared limitations

  • Recording practices vary by team → scores are ordinal signals, not measurements
  • CFR shares its numerator with incident frequency (bounded by sensitivity analysis)
  • Demonstration set, not a portfolio ranking; the contribution is the instrument and its evaluation logic

Robust where it matters

Discrete configurations

  • Temporal 2025 vs 2024: τ = 0.600 / 0.733, tracking real profile change, above all SVC-06’s broad deterioration

98.3%

of all admissible weightings place SVC-03 last, and rank 1 falls to SVC-01, SVC-06 or SVC-04 in every single draw (41%, 32%, 27%). The bottom is weight-independent; the single top rank is a property of the cluster.

8:15–9:15. RQ3 answered: stable at the extremes, sensitive in the middle, and the middle sensitivity is a property of the 2024 data (grouped profiles), not the method, as the flatter 2024 Monte Carlo confirms. The takeaway pre-empts “isn’t temporal instability a flaw?”.

Does it earn its complexity?

DISCRIMINANT VALUE

Against a ranking by incident frequency alone: τ = 0.467 (2025), τ = 0.333 (2024).

SVC-01 rises, fourth to first

Infrastructure lifecycle debt (4,210 unsupported component months, patch recency 63.18) is invisible to an incident count. This is precisely the signal the framework was built to surface.

SVC-05 falls, third to fifth

High incident volume (171/yr) offset by full patch recency and zero unsupported components: noisy, but structurally sound.

The divergence is not noise; it concentrates exactly where multi-criteria measurement should add information over a single signal (consistent with Albarak et al., 2022).

9:15–10:00. Short slide, one idea: if the framework merely reproduced the incident count, it would be redundant. It does not, and the two services that move are the ones that should move. SVC-01 jumps three places on structural exposure alone.

Where the methods disagree, and why that is a finding

CONVERGENT VALIDITY · §5.6

In 2025 all methods agree exactly under equal weights, with SVC-01 first, and SVC-03 is last everywhere. The one disagreement is the 2024 equal-weights top rank, and it is a photo-finish.

2024, equal weights TOPSIS classic SAW · VIKOR · TOPSIS absolute
Top-ranked service SVC-04 (C* 0.4847) SVC-01
Margin ΔC* = 0.004 to SVC-01 (0.4886) Each worst on exactly 2 criteria
Agreement vs SAW τ = 0.600 absolute vs VIKOR τ = 1.000
2025 and 2024 adjusted SVC-01 first under every method

Mechanism: both are worst on exactly two criteria. SVC-04 is perfect on the other two; SVC-01 is only good. Linear aggregation puts SVC-01 first; vector normalisation, preserving SVC-04’s outlying 410 incidents, reverses the pair by 0.004.

10:00–11:15. The distinctive finding. The 2024 equal-weight top rank is a photo-finish: classic TOPSIS puts SVC-04 first by 0.004; SAW, VIKOR and the absolute variant put SVC-01 first, because regret capping and fixed anchors register SVC-01’s worst patch recency and 4,054 unsupported months at full strength while vector normalisation is dominated by SVC-04’s incident mass. In 2025 and under 2024 adjusted weights everything agrees on SVC-01.

Division of labour, not a winner

METHODOLOGICAL CONTRIBUTION

Standard TOPSIS + SAW/VIKOR

  • Primary prioritisation signal
  • Triangulation, not a single number: where scorers split by 0.004, the margin is too small to carry a remediation decision

Absolute-mode TOPSIS

  • Rank-reversal free by construction: every leave-one-out removal preserved, where the classic top rank flips (García-Cascales and Lamata, 2012; Yang, 2020)
  • Scores comparable across observation periods, uniquely among the methods used
  • Complementary perspective; also powers the Monte Carlo cleanly
The single top rank is contested within the SVC-01, SVC-04, SVC-06 cluster across the weight space: a compensability judgement the framework informs but does not settle, and saying so is a feature.

11:15–12:00. This reframing, disagreement as diagnostic rather than failure, is the transferable design lesson beyond the case. The takeaway line doubles as the answer to “so which service is actually first?”.

Anticipated questions

Anticipated questions · 1 of 2

Only six services: what can that prove?
The contribution is the instrument and evaluation logic, not a portfolio ranking. Selection was by data completeness; p-values are exact over all 720 permutations and read as effect sizes; portfolio-wide deployment is stated future work.
You built and evaluated on the same data. Circular?
Declared openly: the evaluation establishes robustness, method-independence and discriminant value, not criterion validity, because no independent measure of non-code debt exists. External validation is the primary future-work avenue.
Are DORA metrics really debt proxies?
Treated as the central testable hypothesis, never a premise. Forsgren, Humble and Kim (2018) validate the metrics; Nord, Ozkaya and Woody (2021) ground the diagnostic reading; the rankings match literature-predicted profiles. Longitudinal tracking would move it from plausible to evidenced.

12:00–13:15. Deliver these deadpan; each answer is one breath. The move being made: every hard question already has a stated, chapter-referenced answer in the thesis (Chapters 3–6), so the defence is consistent with the document.

Anticipated questions, continued

Anticipated questions · 2 of 2

Aren’t the differential weights arbitrary?
Yes, and they are declared as a practitioner assumption. That is why the evaluation does not depend on them: across 20,000 Dirichlet-sampled weightings the lowest priority holds in 98.3% of draws and rank 1 stays inside the same three-service cluster in all of them.
Could this be misused to judge teams?
Governance scope was agreed with the Head of Trading IT at the outset: decision-support for portfolio conversations, not a binding instrument. High-debt profiles typically reflect historical design decisions, not current custodians (Ahmad et al., 2026). No team, vendor or individual identifiers exist in the dataset.
The framework measures debt indirectly and expresses the result as a relative priority. “Measuring” in the title means a quantitative, reproducible ordering, not a claim to have quantified the debt itself.

13:15–14:00. The takeaway restates the scope sentence from Chapter 1 verbatim in spirit; ending the pre-emption block on it shows the claim discipline has been consistent from the first page to the last.

Contributions

CHAPTER 6

  1. First application (to the best of current knowledge) of TOPSIS to live ITSM/CMDB records for TD prioritisation in financial trading IT, closing the gap Lenarduzzi et al. (2021) identify field-wide
