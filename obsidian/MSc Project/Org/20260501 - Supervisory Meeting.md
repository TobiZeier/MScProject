## 2. Q&A Session
Tobi asked several questions about metrics, weighting, validation, the next chapter, and the defence.

### Metrics

**Answer:** State that no specific metrics were found in the literature review, but explain why the chosen metrics were selected.

### Weighting
**Question:** There is no evidence on which metric is more important, so weight values are assigned based on best practice.

**Answer:** That is acceptable, and the literature should be cited accordingly.

### Validation
**Question:** How can the score be validated for accuracy?

**Answer:** Use weight perturbation to check whether the ranking is stable. Add a disclaimer stating that the weight assignment does not work in every scenario or application area. Use 2024 data to create the score, then check 2025 incident data to see whether the score is accurate.

### Next chapter
**Question:** Is there a specific direction for the next chapter?

**Answer:** Guidance can be given once a draft is available.

### Defence
**Question:** How does the defence work, and what should be expected?

**Answer:** Consider the pillar analogy for your thesis. These pillars represent the problem statement, and your defense serves to protect their stability. If a reviewer dismantles a single pillar by exposing a fundamental flaw in your problem identification, the overarching structure begins to fracture. Should multiple pillars fail, the roof collapses and your proposed solution loses its validity. Ultimately, the success of the thesis depends upon demonstrating that the identified problem is both authentic and significant.
