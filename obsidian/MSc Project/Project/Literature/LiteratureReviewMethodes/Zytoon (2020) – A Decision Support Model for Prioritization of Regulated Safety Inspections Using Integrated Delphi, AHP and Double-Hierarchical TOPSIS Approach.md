## Summary
Technical study developing a multi stage decision support model to prioritise Occupational Safety and Health (OSH) inspections. The researchers combine three distinct methodologies; Delphi, Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), and Double Hierarchical TOPSIS (DH-TOPSIS) to create a robust framework that ranks industrial firms based on four primary criteria: hazard level, safety performance, firm characteristics, and inspection history. The model aim is to optimise limited inspection resources by targeting high risk entities more accurately than traditional methods.

## Key Arguments
- Current OSH inspection planning often lacks the efficiency required to significantly reduce injury rates due to subjective or simplistic prioritisation
- Effective inspection targeting requires a hierarchical approach that can handle both qualitative expert consensus and quantitative performance data
- The integration of AHP for criteria weighting and DH-TOPSIS for final ranking provides a mathematically rigorous way to handle the complexity of industrial safety variables
- Systematising the decision process reduces the influence of individual inspector bias and ensures resource allocation is driven by empirical risk indicators

## Key Concepts
- Integrated Delphi AHP approach
- Double Hierarchical TOPSIS (DH-TOPSIS)
- Multi Criteria Decision Making (MCDM)
- OSH inspection prioritisation
- Relative closeness coefficient
