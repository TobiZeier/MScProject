## Summary
Repaying documentation debt is structurally more complex than anticipated because documentation debt is entangled with architectural and people debt in mutually reinforcing feedback loops. The process involves four phases (realisation, resourcing, documenting, evaluation) and requires ongoing iterative activity rather than a one-time project. The absence of systematic prioritisation was identified as a critical failure mode.

## Key Findings
- Documentation debt cannot be resolved independently, architectural and people debt create interdependencies that block purely documentary solutions
- The case company performed no systematic prioritisation of documentation tasks, defaulting to volume over strategic value under time pressure, identified in hindsight as a significant strategic error
- "Good enough" documentation thresholds are difficult to define in advance, especially in highly customised systems
- Documentation debt is a form of IT risk requiring active IT governance, not just developer discipline
- People debt (siloed competencies, key person dependencies) compounds documentation debt when knowledge holders leave

## Method
Action research; single company case study; qualitative data collection over extended period; practitioner-oriented output

## Relevance to Thesis
- Section 2.6.2 (The Prioritisation Gap): Concrete empirical instance of prioritisation absence within TD repayment, the company's failure to prioritise documentation tasks resulted in wasted effort and low-utility output
- Section 2.8.1 (The Case for Multi-Criteria Methods): The entanglement of documentation, architectural, and people debt demonstrates why single-type TD approaches are insufficient, a multi-criteria view is necessary

## Critical Notes
- Single-company action research, very limited generalisability
- Focused exclusively on documentation debt, one of the least quantifiable TD types; cannot be extended to infrastructure or architectural TD without qualification
- Does not engage with operational metrics, service-level measurement, or quantitative prioritisation at all
- Narrow scope means it should be cited selectively and not used to make broad claims about TD repayment generally
