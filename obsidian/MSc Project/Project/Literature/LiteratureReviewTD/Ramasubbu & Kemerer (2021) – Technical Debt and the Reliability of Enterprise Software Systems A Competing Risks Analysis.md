## Summary
Empirical study investigating technical debt (TD) remediation within the context of outsourced enterprise systems. Analysing data from 1,824 real world projects, the authors examine how "control balancing", the periodic adjustment of formal and informal management controls, affects the ability of client and vendor teams to mitigate TD. The research finds that while control balancing is generally beneficial, its effectiveness is significantly enhanced when clear processes exist for migrating debt laden systems to new technological platforms.

## Key Arguments
- TD remediation in outsourcing is uniquely difficult due to information asymmetry between the client who owns the system and the vendor who maintains it
- Fixed control configurations often fail as project conditions change; "balancing" between outcome, process, and social controls is necessary to align incentives
- Control balancing acts as a governance mechanism that helps surface "hidden" debt that vendors might otherwise ignore in favour of new feature delivery
- The positive impact of management controls on TD is non-linear and depends heavily on the technical maturity of the migration path available to the team

## Key Concepts
- Control balancing
- Information asymmetry
- TD remediation
- Formal vs Informal controls
- Outsourced maintenance governance
